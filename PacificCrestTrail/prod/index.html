What to Know About Callahan's Lodge, Oregon

Callahan's Lodge sits directly on the PCT at 4100 ft elevation. June 27 falls in peak thru-hiker season with steady northbound traffic. The lodge offers resupply, showers, and meals.

Local customs: PCT hikers remove boots before entering the lodge common areas.

Best times of day: Early morning on the trail avoids afternoon heat on south-facing slopes.

Transportation quirks: No public transit reaches the lodge; arrange pickup or continue on foot.

Safety: Watch for rattlesnakes on sunny trail sections after 10am.

Crowd patterns: Lodge fills with thru-hikers each evening; arrive by mid-afternoon for a bed.

Local etiquette: Pack out all trash; the nearest dumpster is at the lodge.

What to Know About Fish Lake Resort, Oregon

Fish Lake Resort functions primarily as a PCT resupply and lodging stop with limited services. July weather is generally stable but afternoon thunderstorms can form over the Cascades. The single-day stop allows time for a short out-and-back on the PCT before continuing north.

Local customs: PCT hikers are expected to pack out all trash and keep noise low near the resort cabins after 9 pm.

Best times of day: Early morning offers the coolest temperatures and clearest skies for any climb toward Mount McLoughlin.

Transportation quirks: No public transit reaches the resort; arrival and departure are on foot via the PCT only.

Safety: Black bears are active around the lake; store food in provided bear boxes at the resort.

Crowd patterns: Mid-week arrivals in early July see fewer than ten hikers per night at the resort.

Local etiquette: Yield to equestrians on the PCT and step off trail to let horses pass.

What to Know About Elk Lake Resort, Oregon

Elk Lake Resort operates as a PCT resupply point with a small store and limited lodging. July is peak season with full occupancy on weekends. Single-day stop allows morning arrival and afternoon hiking before overnight.

Local customs: PCT hikers stage packs outside the store while shopping. Quiet hours begin at 10pm around the resort cabins.

Best times of day: Early morning offers calm water on Elk Lake before boat traffic. Late afternoon provides cooler temperatures for PCT walking.

Transportation quirks: No public transit reaches the resort. The nearest services are 18 miles north in Bend.

Safety: Afternoon thunderstorms are common in July. Carry rain gear and descend from exposed ridges if lightning approaches.

Crowd patterns: Day visitors peak between 11am and 3pm. Overnight PCT hikers arrive and depart mainly in morning hours.

Local etiquette: Yield to horses on the PCT. Pack out all trash including food wrappers from the lakeshore.

What to Know About Cascade Locks, Oregon

Cascade Locks sits on the Columbia River at the lowest point on the Oregon PCT. August days are warm with low humidity; carry extra water on trail sections above town. Two-day stay allows one full hiking day and one rest day.

Local customs: PCT hikers commonly resupply at the small market and use the post office for bounce boxes.

Best times of day: Early morning starts avoid afternoon heat on exposed ridge sections. River breezes pick up after 3 pm.

Transportation quirks: No public transit; the town is compact enough to walk between lodging, food, and the PCT trailhead.

Safety: Watch for rattlesnakes on south-facing slopes. River currents are strong near the locks.

Crowd patterns: Fewer through-hikers than July; weekend day visitors increase at the Marine Park.

Local etiquette: Yield to equestrians on the PCT. Pack out all trash; no trash cans on trail.
