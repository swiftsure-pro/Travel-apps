St. George, Utah

October 17, 2026

Paul Torres

What to Know About St. George, Utah

St. George has a rich cultural scene in October, with opportunities to enjoy local art and music. The area features galleries, outdoor concerts, and community gatherings. Visitors can explore the natural beauty of the region while engaging with the local culture through various informal events.

Local tip: Check out the local farmers market on Saturday mornings for fresh produce and artisan goods. It's a great way to experience the community vibe. More info

Claire Press

Jake Nackos

Joshua Hoehne

🧥 What to Expect

Typical October temperatures are around 77°F daytime and 55°F overnight. The desert landscape of St. George displays warm autumn hues with cottonwoods turning golden. Crowds are lighter than summer, making it easier to explore. Most attractions are open with no significant seasonal closures.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Snow Canyon State Park
Must-SeeModerate2–3 hrs
Snow Canyon features red and white sandstone cliffs with unique lava tubes. The trails vary in elevation, with some leading to stunning overlooks. Best explored in the afternoon light.📌 A day-use fee is required for entry.
📍Pioneer Park
Easy1 hr
Pioneer Park features scenic views of the surrounding area and several short trails. The park is a great place for a leisurely stroll.
🥾Red Cliffs Desert Reserve
Easy1–2 hrs
The reserve offers easy trails through striking red rock formations and desert landscapes. Look for unique wildlife and plants native to the area.
🎯Sand Hollow State Park
2–4 hrs
This park features a large reservoir ideal for boating, fishing, and swimming. The sandy beaches and surrounding dunes are perfect for relaxation.
🏛️St. George Dinosaur Discovery Site
1–2 hrs
This site showcases fossilized dinosaur tracks and a variety of exhibits about prehistoric life. Interactive displays make it engaging for all ages.
🗻St. George Tabernacle
30 min
This historic building showcases beautiful architecture and offers insights into the area's history. Guided tours are available.
🏛️Tuacahn Amphitheatre
2–3 hrs
Tuacahn hosts outdoor performances in a stunning canyon setting. Check the schedule for shows that may be available during your visit.
🚗Zion Canyon Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive6 miles one-way
Zion Canyon Scenic Drive winds through towering sandstone cliffs and lush greenery. Key viewpoints include Court of the Patriarchs and Weeping Rock, both offering unique perspectives of the canyon's geology. The drive is closed to private vehicles during peak season; a shuttle is required. In October, enjoy cooler temperatures and fall colors in the canyon, enhancing the landscape's rich hues.
🚗Kolob Canyons Road
Scenic Drive5 miles one-way
Kolob Canyons Road features striking red rock formations and expansive views of the canyon. Highlights include the Timber Creek Overlook and the scenic pullouts that offer views of the surrounding mountains. The road is paved and suitable for all vehicles, with ample parking at the viewpoints. October provides clear skies and vibrant fall foliage, making the scenery particularly inviting.
🚗Cedar Mountain Scenic Byway
Day Trip45 miles one-way
Cedar Mountain Scenic Byway traverses diverse landscapes, from red rock formations to pine forests. Along the route, stop at the Cedar Breaks National Monument for views of the amphitheater-like erosion features. The drive is mostly paved with sections requiring caution due to elevation changes. In October, enjoy the contrast of fall colors against the evergreen backdrop, with cooler temperatures ideal for a comfortable drive.
🚗Observation Point
Viewpoint1-hour drive from St. George
Observation Point offers panoramic views of Zion Canyon, including iconic formations like Angels Landing and the Great White Throne. The viewpoint requires a short hike from the parking area, but the effort is rewarded with a stunning vista. Parking is available at the trailhead, but it fills quickly in October. The fall season enhances the colors of the foliage, framing the dramatic rock formations beautifully.

Zion National Park

October 18, 2026

NPS / Ally O'Rullian

What to Know About Zion National Park

Zion National Park has a remote setting with limited cultural events in October. Visitors can engage with the park's natural beauty through ranger-led programs, evening talks at the amphitheater, and exhibits at the visitor center. The nearest gateway town, Springdale, offers a few local dining options and occasional live music at restaurants, but specific events may not be scheduled during this time.

Local tip: Check out the evening programs at the Zion National Park amphitheater. These programs typically feature ranger talks and are a great way to learn about the park's ecology and history. More info

NPS / Ally O'Rullian

🧥 What to Expect

Typical October temperatures are around 62°F daytime and 38°F overnight. Autumn colors enhance the red rock landscape, with cottonwoods and maples displaying rich yellows and oranges. Crowds will be lower than during summer peak season. The park shuttle is necessary for access to Zion Canyon, with service beginning early in the morning and ending around sunset.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Angels Landing
Must-SeeStrenuous4–5 hrs round-trip
The trail features a steep ascent with a chain-assisted section along a narrow ridge. This hike covers 5 miles with a 1,488-ft elevation gain. A permit is required for day hikes, obtained via lottery.📌 Permit required for day hikes.
🥾The Narrows
Must-SeeModerate3–8 hrs depending on route
This hike follows the Virgin River through a slot canyon, with options to turn back at various points. The round-trip distance can vary significantly based on how far you go. Water shoes are essential due to the river conditions.📌 Water shoes essential for the hike.
🥾Canyon Overlook Trail
Moderate1 hr round-trip
A short but scenic trail leading to a viewpoint that overlooks the main canyon. The trail is 1 mile long with some steep drop-offs along the edges. Ideal for sunset views.📌 Parking is limited; arrive early.
🥾Emerald Pools Trail
Easy1.5–2 hrs round-trip
This trail features a series of pools and waterfalls with lush vegetation. The hike is about 1.2 miles long with minimal elevation gain. Best visited in the morning to avoid crowds.📌 Access via shuttle only.
🥾Weeping Rock
Easy30 min
A short, steep trail leads to a rock alcove with dripping springs and hanging gardens. The trail is only 0.4 miles round-trip. Trail may close due to rockfall danger.📌 Trail may be closed temporarily.
📍Kolob Canyons
1–2 hrs for scenic drive
This area features stunning red rock formations and a scenic drive with multiple viewpoints. The main road is 5 miles long, offering several pullouts for photos.📌 Access via the Kolob Canyons Road.
🚗Zion Canyon Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive6 miles one-way
Zion Canyon Scenic Drive winds through towering sandstone cliffs and lush vegetation. Key viewpoints include Court of the Patriarchs and Weeping Rock, both accessible by short walks from the road. The park shuttle operates here; private vehicles are not allowed during peak hours. In October, the fall colors enhance the landscape, with vibrant reds and yellows contrasting against the warm tones of the rock.
🚗Kolob Canyons Road
Scenic Drive5 miles one-way
Kolob Canyons Road offers a panoramic view of the dramatic red cliffs and narrow canyons. Notable stops include the Timber Creek Overlook, where you can see the expanse of the canyons below. This road is less crowded, making it ideal for a peaceful drive. In October, the cooler temperatures and changing leaves add to the allure of this short drive, enhancing the rich colors of the landscape.
🚗Zion-Mount Carmel Highway
Day Trip10 miles one-way
Zion-Mount Carmel Highway connects Zion National Park with the east entrance. This drive features stunning vistas of steep cliffs and the famous Checkerboard Mesa. Pull-outs along the way allow for quick stops to take in the scenery. Be cautious of steep drop-offs and tight turns. In October, the highway offers clear views with fewer tourists, making for a relaxing drive.

🧥 What to Expect

Typical October temperatures are around 60°F daytime and 40°F overnight. October brings vivid hues to Bryce Canyon as the aspen leaves turn gold against the red rock formations. Crowds are lighter than in summer, making for a more serene experience on the trails. The park does not require shuttle reservations in October, but parking may fill up early at popular trailheads.

🚗 Getting Here

Open in Google Maps →
Zion NP → Bryce Canyon NP
95 mi 2 hrs 15 min

Travel northeast on US-89 from Zion National Park, enjoying views of striking landscapes. The final approach to Bryce Canyon features scenic overlooks that showcase the unique hoodoo formations. Be aware that parking at the park entrance can be limited during peak hours.

🧭 CAN'T-MISS ENROUTE
🥾
Red Canyon (3 mi detour | 5 min)
This scenic area features striking red rock formations and a short 1-mile trail through the canyon. Stop here for a quick hike to stretch your legs and enjoy the views.
🏛️
Panguitch (5 mi detour | 10 min)
A charming small town known for its picturesque main street and local diners. It's a great spot to grab a coffee or snack before heading to the park.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Navajo Loop Trail
Must-SeeModerate1.5–2 hrs round-trip
This 1.3-mile loop descends through Wall Street, a narrow canyon flanked by towering hoodoos. Expect a series of switchbacks and a 500-ft elevation change. Best explored early in the day to avoid crowds.📌 No permit required.
📍Bryce Point
Must-See30 min
One of the highest viewpoints in the park, offering expansive views over the Bryce Amphitheater. The vista reveals a complex landscape of rock towers and formations.📌 Parking limited; arrive early.
🥾Fairyland Loop
Strenuous4–5 hrs round-trip
This 8-mile loop features stunning views of unique rock formations and a more secluded experience. Expect some steep sections with an elevation gain of 1,000 ft.📌 No permit required.
🥾Queens Garden Trail
Easy2–3 hrs round-trip
This 1.8-mile trail offers a gentle descent into the canyon, leading to unique rock formations and lush vegetation. The elevation gain is about 320 ft, making it accessible for most hikers.📌 No permit required.
🎯Dark Sky Stargazing
2–3 hrs
Bryce Canyon is designated as a Dark Sky Park, making it perfect for stargazing. Clear October skies offer excellent visibility of celestial events.📌 Bring a blanket and warm clothing for comfort.
📍Sunrise Point
30 min
A popular viewpoint that showcases the vibrant colors of the canyon at dawn. The view from here includes a panoramic look at the hoodoos below.📌 Best visited at sunrise for stunning views.
🚗Bryce Canyon Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive18 miles one-way
Bryce Canyon Scenic Drive traverses the park, offering views of unique hoodoo formations carved from the red rock. Key viewpoints include Sunrise Point, Sunset Point, and Inspiration Point, each highlighting different aspects of the canyon's colorful landscape. The drive has multiple pull-outs for easy parking and photography opportunities. October provides clear skies and crisp air, enhancing visibility of the vibrant rock formations.
🚗Fairyland Point
Viewpoint10-min drive from park entrance
Fairyland Point offers a panoramic view of Bryce Canyon's intricate hoodoos and spires, stretching out towards the horizon. From this vantage point, look for notable formations like the Sinking Ship and the Castle. There is a parking area available directly at the viewpoint. Visiting in October means fewer crowds and the chance to see the canyon walls reflecting the warm colors of the setting sun.
🚗Scenic Byway 12
Day Trip124-mile loop — allow a full day
Scenic Byway 12 is a breathtaking drive that connects Bryce Canyon National Park to other scenic areas, including Red Canyon and Grand Staircase-Escalante. The route features dramatic elevations and sweeping views of canyons and rock formations. Key stops include the overlook at the top of the Escalante River and Red Canyon. The road is well-maintained, but check for seasonal weather conditions before traveling. October's fall foliage adds vibrant colors to the landscape.

Capitol Reef National Park

October 21–22, 2026

NPS/Pete Wintersteen

What to Know About Capitol Reef National Park

Capitol Reef National Park is a remote destination with a focus on natural beauty and outdoor activities. While specific ticketed events may not be available during your visit, the park offers ranger-led programs, evening talks, and interpretive exhibits at the visitor center. The nearby town of Torrey provides a small selection of local activities and community gatherings.

Local tip: Check for ranger-led programs at the Capitol Reef Visitor Center, which typically occur daily and provide insights into the park's natural and cultural history. More info

NPS / Pete Wintersteen

🧥 What to Expect

Typical October temperatures are around 61°F daytime and 41°F overnight. October brings colorful fall foliage to Capitol Reef, with bright reds and oranges contrasting the sandstone cliffs. Crowds are lighter than summer, making for a more peaceful experience. Most trails are in good condition, but be aware that some higher elevations may have lingering snow.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Hickman Bridge
Must-SeeModerate2 hrs round-trip
The trail to Hickman Bridge is 2 miles with an elevation gain of 400 feet. The natural bridge spans 133 feet and offers excellent views of the surrounding cliffs. Best approached in the morning for cooler temperatures.📌 No permit required.
📍Goosenecks Overlook
Must-See30 min
This viewpoint provides sweeping vistas of the meandering river below and the dramatic canyon formations. It's particularly beautiful during sunset.📌 Accessible by car; no permit required.
🥾Cassidy Arch
Moderate3–4 hrs round-trip
A 3.5-mile trail leads to Cassidy Arch, which is 130 feet wide. The route involves a steep section but rewards hikers with stunning views of the arch and surrounding landscape.📌 No permit required.
🥾Grand Wash Trail
Easy2–3 hrs round-trip
This 2.2-mile hike features towering canyon walls and unique rock formations. The trail is mostly flat, making it accessible for all skill levels. Watch for rockfall warnings, especially after rain.📌 No permit required.
🏛️Fremont Petroglyphs
30 min
This short, accessible site features ancient petroglyphs carved into the rock by the Fremont people. The interpretive signs provide context to the artwork.📌 No permit required.
🏛️Fruita Historic District
1 hr
Fruita is known for its historic buildings and fruit orchards. The area offers a glimpse into early settler life and features picnic areas.📌 Visit the historic Gifford Homestead for fresh baked goods.
🏛️Gifford Homestead
1 hr
The Gifford Homestead offers a chance to see a preserved homestead and sample homemade pies and jams. The setting is picturesque, surrounded by orchards.📌 Open seasonally; check hours before visiting.
🚗Capitol Reef Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive8 miles one-way
Capitol Reef Scenic Drive traverses a diverse landscape of red rock formations, canyons, and lush orchards. Key stops include the Gifford Homestead at Mile 2.5 and the Goosenecks Overlook at Mile 4.5, where you can see the meandering Sulphur Creek. The drive is paved and suitable for all vehicles, but parking can be limited during peak hours. In October, the soft light enhances the colors of the landscape, and the orchards may be ripe for fruit picking.
🚗Chimney Rock Viewpoint
Viewpoint15-min drive from visitor center
Chimney Rock Viewpoint offers a commanding view of the unique monolithic formation rising above the surrounding landscape. From this overlook, you can see the Waterpocket Fold and the distant Henry Mountains. The viewpoint has a small parking area, but it can fill quickly, so arrive early. In October, the setting sun casts warm tones on the rock, making the view particularly photogenic.
🚗Hickman Bridge Viewpoint
Viewpoint30-min drive from visitor center
Hickman Bridge Viewpoint provides views of a natural rock arch that spans 133 feet. The viewpoint is accessible via a short walk from the parking area, and you can see the Fremont River and surrounding cliffs. There is a designated parking lot, but it can become crowded. In October, the cooler temperatures make the short hike pleasant, and the fall foliage adds color to the scenery.
