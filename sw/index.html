St. George, Utah

October 17, 2026

Paul Torres

What to Know About St. George, Utah

St. George has a lively cultural scene in October, with various local events and activities. The community often hosts outdoor concerts, art walks, and fall harvest festivals. Visitors can enjoy the local arts scene and explore the area's natural beauty during this time.

Local tip: Check out the local farmers market on Saturday mornings for fresh produce and handmade goods. It's a great way to experience the community and support local vendors. More info

Claire Press

Jake Nackos

Joshua Hoehne

🧥 What to Expect

Typical October temperatures are around 77°F daytime and 55°F overnight. The desert landscape is marked by vibrant fall colors among the red rock formations. Expect moderate crowds as this period is less busy than summer months. Most trails are open, but park facilities may have limited hours.

🚗 Getting Here

Open in Google Maps →
Las Vegas, NV → St. George, Utah

Take I-15 S to I-15 S towards St. George. The drive features a scenic descent into the Virgin River Valley. Upon arrival, expect ample parking near key attractions.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Snow Canyon State Park
Must-SeeModerate2–3 hrs
Explore 7 miles of hiking trails among lava tubes and red rock formations. Key highlights include the Petrified Dunes and views of the canyon. Ideal for mid-morning visits to avoid afternoon heat.📌 An entrance fee is required for park access.
🥾Red Cliffs Desert Reserve
Moderate2–4 hrs
Hike through a desert landscape featuring red cliffs and unique geological formations. The trail is approximately 5 miles long and includes a stream crossing. Best visited in the morning for cooler temperatures.
📍Pioneer Park
Easy1 hr
This park features unique rock formations and offers excellent views of St. George. A short, accessible trail leads to several overlooks. Perfect for a quick stop or picnic.
🏛️St. George Dinosaur Discovery Site
1–2 hrs
This site features well-preserved dinosaur tracks and fossils in an indoor museum setting. Interactive exhibits make it a great stop for families. Plan for a leisurely visit; guided tours may be available.
🏛️St. George Historic District
1–2 hrs
Explore historic buildings and homes dating back to the 1800s. A self-guided walking tour provides insight into the area's history. Great opportunity for photography.
🏛️Tuacahn Amphitheatre
2–3 hrs
An outdoor amphitheater hosting various performances and concerts. The backdrop of red rock cliffs enhances the experience. Check the schedule for evening shows.
🚗Zion Canyon Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive6 miles one-way
Zion Canyon Scenic Drive travels through a deep, narrow canyon, bordered by towering sandstone cliffs. Key stops include Court of the Patriarchs and Zion Lodge, with parking available at designated areas. The section beyond the Temple of Sinawava is accessible by shuttle only during peak season. October offers cooler temperatures and vibrant fall foliage, making it an ideal time for photography and exploration.
🚗Kolob Terrace Road
Day Trip20 miles one-way
Kolob Terrace Road winds through diverse landscapes, from high plateaus to lush canyons. Key viewpoints include the Timber Creek Overlook and the entry to Kolob Canyons. The road is paved but features some narrow sections; watch for steep drop-offs. October provides clear skies and crisp air, enhancing visibility of the colorful fall foliage across the terrain.
🚗Observation Point
Viewpoint1-hour drive from St. George, plus a 8-mile round-trip hike
Observation Point offers a sweeping view of Zion Canyon, including Angels Landing and the Virgin River below. The viewpoint requires a hike, with steep elevation gain, but the panoramic vistas are worth the effort. Parking is available at the East Rim trailhead, but arrive early to secure a spot. October’s mild temperatures make the hike more enjoyable and the fall colors highlight the canyon’s features.
🚗Cedar Breaks Scenic Byway
Scenic Drive40 miles one-way
Cedar Breaks Scenic Byway traverses high-elevation terrain with views of colorful rock formations and the expansive Cedar Breaks National Monument. Key overlooks include the Point Supreme and the North View overlook. The road surface is paved but can be narrow in sections. October brings cooler temperatures and fewer visitors, making it an excellent time for a leisurely drive and photography.

Zion National Park

October 18, 2026

NPS / Ally O'Rullian

What to Know About Zion National Park

Zion National Park is a remote destination with a focus on natural beauty and outdoor activities rather than ticketed cultural events. In October, visitors can participate in ranger-led programs, including talks and guided hikes, which provide insights into the park's ecology and history. The nearby town of Springdale offers a few local experiences, such as art galleries and occasional live music at local venues.

Local tip: Check out the Springdale Farmers Market, which typically operates on Saturdays. It's a great place to find local produce and crafts, and it gives a taste of the community's character. More info

NPS / Ally O'Rullian

🧥 What to Expect

Typical October temperatures are around 62°F daytime and 38°F overnight. The canyon walls will display vibrant autumn colors, with reds and oranges reflected in the Virgin River. Crowds will be lower than during the summer peak season, making for a more pleasant experience. The park shuttle is required for access to popular areas like Zion Canyon, so plan accordingly.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Angels Landing
Must-SeeStrenuous4–5 hrs round-trip
The trail climbs 1,488 feet over 5 miles, featuring steep switchbacks and a narrow ridge with chains for support. Start early to avoid crowds and enjoy the cooler morning temperatures.📌 A permit is required, obtainable through a day-before lottery.
🥾The Narrows
Must-SeeModerate3–8 hrs round-trip
This hike follows the Virgin River through a narrow slot canyon, with towering walls rising over 1,000 feet. Water levels can vary; check conditions before starting and consider water shoes.📌 Shuttle access is required; check the water conditions before visiting.
🥾Canyon Overlook Trail
Moderate1 hr
A 1-mile round-trip trail with stunning panoramic views of Zion Canyon. The trail includes some rocky sections and elevation gain of about 200 feet.📌 Access is via the Zion-Mount Carmel Highway; parking is limited.
🥾Emerald Pools Trail
Easy1–2 hrs round-trip
A short, family-friendly trail leading to beautiful pools and waterfalls with an elevation gain of 200 feet. This trail can be busy, so visit early or late in the day.📌 No permit required.
🥾Weeping Rock
Easy30 min
A short, steep hike to an alcove with hanging gardens and dripping springs. The trail is less than a half-mile round-trip, making it accessible for most visitors.📌 Trail access may be closed due to rockfall; check for updates.
🏛️Kolob Canyons
1–2 hrs
A scenic area featuring striking red cliffs and a 5-mile scenic drive. The area is less frequented than the main park, offering a quieter experience.📌 No shuttle service; accessible by vehicle year-round.
🚗Zion Canyon Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive6 miles one-way
Zion Canyon Scenic Drive winds through towering sandstone cliffs and lush vegetation. Key viewpoints include Court of the Patriarchs at mile 1.5 and Zion Lodge at mile 3. A shuttle is required during peak season, but private vehicles are allowed in October. The softer light of fall enhances the warm colors of the rock faces, making it an excellent time for photography.
🚗Kolob Canyons Road
Scenic Drive5 miles one-way
Kolob Canyons Road provides access to striking red rock formations and panoramic views of the surrounding wilderness. Key stops include the Timber Creek Overlook at the end of the drive, which offers sweeping views to the east. The road is narrow with limited parking at some pull-outs. The fall foliage adds vibrant colors to the landscape, making October a picturesque time to visit.
🚗Observation Point
Viewpoint30-min drive from Zion Canyon
Observation Point presents a breathtaking view of Zion Canyon, including the iconic Angel's Landing. From the viewpoint, you can see the valley below and the surrounding peaks that rise sharply. Parking is available at the trailhead, but it can fill up quickly in October. The fall colors enhance the beauty of the view, making the hike to this point rewarding during this month.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Navajo Loop Trail
Must-SeeModerate1.5–2 hrs round-trip
The trail descends into the canyon, showcasing iconic hoodoos and rock formations. It features steep switchbacks and a total distance of 1.3 miles. Best explored early to avoid afternoon crowds.📌 No permit is required to hike this trail.
🥾Queens Garden Trail
Must-SeeEasy2–3 hrs round-trip
This 1.8-mile trail leads visitors through a stunning array of hoodoos, with gentle elevation changes. The trail offers various viewpoints and is ideal for families.📌 No permit is required to hike this trail.
🥾Fairyland Loop
Strenuous4–5 hrs round-trip
A challenging 8-mile loop that traverses less-visited areas of the park, showcasing diverse rock formations and panoramic views. Expect a total elevation gain of 1,000 feet.📌 Start early to avoid strong afternoon sun.
📍Bryce Point
30 min
This viewpoint provides sweeping vistas of the canyon, particularly beautiful during sunset. The viewpoint is easily accessible and offers benches for resting.📌 Parking is limited; arrive early.
🎯Dark Sky Stargazing
2–3 hrs
Bryce Canyon is a designated Dark Sky Park, making it an ideal location for stargazing. The clear October skies offer excellent visibility of constellations and celestial events.📌 Bring a blanket and warm clothing for evening temperatures.
📍Sunrise Point
30 min
A popular viewpoint that offers a stunning panorama of the canyon at sunrise. It provides ample photo opportunities of the changing colors on the hoodoos.📌 Arrive early for the best sunrise views.
🚗Bryce Canyon Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive18 miles one-way
Bryce Canyon Scenic Drive winds through a series of viewpoints overlooking the park's unique hoodoo formations. Key stops include Sunrise Point at mile 1.0, which offers early light on the spires, and Rainbow Point at mile 18.0, the highest point in the park with views of the Paunsaugunt Plateau. The road is paved and suitable for all vehicles, but parking can fill quickly at popular viewpoints. October brings fewer crowds and cooler temperatures, enhancing the experience of the vibrant fall colors against the red rock.
🚗Inspiration Point
Viewpoint15-min drive from park entrance
Inspiration Point provides an expansive view of Bryce Canyon's amphitheater, showcasing a dramatic array of hoodoos and cliffs. The viewpoint looks southeast, displaying formations like the Silent City and the Two Bridges. A short walk from the parking area leads to the viewpoint, which has limited parking spaces. October's clear skies and crisp air make the colors pop, creating an ideal setting for photography.
🚗Scenic Byway 12
Day Trip124-mile loop — allow a full day
Scenic Byway 12 takes you through diverse landscapes, including red rock canyons, forests, and rolling hills. Begin at Bryce Canyon and travel toward Escalante, with notable stops at Red Canyon and the Escalante River. The drive features numerous pull-outs for photos and short walks. Be aware of the winding roads and occasional steep grades. In October, the fall foliage adds richness to the scenery, making this drive particularly rewarding.

Capitol Reef National Park

October 21–22, 2026

NPS/Pete Wintersteen

What to Know About Capitol Reef National Park

Capitol Reef National Park is a remote destination with a focus on natural beauty and outdoor activities. In October, visitors can engage in the park's interpretive programs, including ranger-led talks and evening programs at the amphitheater. The nearby town of Torrey offers a relaxed atmosphere with occasional local markets and community gatherings.

NPS / Pete Wintersteen

🧥 What to Expect

Typical October temperatures are around 61°F daytime and 41°F overnight. October brings vibrant fall colors to the park's rock formations and foliage. Crowds are thinner than summer, providing a quieter experience. Most trails are accessible, but check for any seasonal closures or conditions at visitor centers.

Current Weather

🚗 Getting Here

Open in Google Maps →
Bryce Canyon NP → Capitol Reef NP
95 mi 1 hr 45 min

Take US-89 S and then UT-24 E for a scenic drive through the Fish Springs area. The road winds through open desert landscapes with occasional views of the Waterpocket Fold, a prominent geological feature. Parking is available at the visitor center upon arrival.

🧭 CAN'T-MISS ENROUTE
🥾
Red Canyon (5 mi detour | 10 min)
This scenic area features striking red rock formations and offers several short hiking trails. A perfect spot for a quick stretch and photos.
🛍️
Torrey, UT (0 mi detour | 0 min)
This small town has local shops and eateries for a quick snack or coffee before entering the park. The rustic charm provides a glimpse into the local culture.

🏔️ Top Attractions

🥾Hickman Bridge
Must-SeeModerate2 hrs round-trip
The trail to Hickman Bridge is 1.8 miles round-trip with an elevation gain of about 400 feet. The arch spans 133 feet and offers great views of the surrounding area. Early morning is the best time to avoid crowds.📌 No permits required for this hike.
🏛️Fruita Historic District
Must-See1 hr
This historic area includes preserved pioneer homesteads and heritage fruit trees. Visitors can explore the old schoolhouse and see seasonal fruit harvests in October. Be sure to check local guidelines for fruit picking.
🥾Cassidy Arch
Strenuous5 hrs round-trip
The hike to Cassidy Arch is 3 miles one way with significant elevation gain. The arch is a massive rock formation that provides dramatic views of the area. Best tackled in cooler weather.📌 Bring plenty of water, as there are limited sources on this trail.
🥾Grand Wash Trail
Moderate3–4 hrs round-trip
This 4.4-mile round-trip trail leads through a narrow canyon with towering cliffs. Look for unique rock formations and petroglyphs along the way. The trail can be muddy after rainfall.📌 Check trail conditions at the visitor center before starting.
🗻Fremont Petroglyphs
30 min
This site features ancient rock carvings created by the Fremont culture. A short walking path leads to the petroglyphs, which are best viewed in the late afternoon when the light enhances visibility.
🏛️Gifford Homestead
30 min
The Gifford Homestead showcases the history of early settlers in the area. A small gift shop offers locally made goods and baked items. Check for opening hours, as they can vary.
📍Goosenecks Overlook
30 min
This viewpoint provides a stunning look at the meandering river below, carved into the landscape. It's an easy walk from the parking area and offers great photo opportunities.
🚗Capitol Reef Scenic Drive
Scenic Drive8 miles one-way
Capitol Reef Scenic Drive winds through the heart of the park, showcasing the unique geology of the Waterpocket Fold. Highlights include the impressive cliffs of the Navajo Sandstone and the historic Fruita district with its orchards. There are several pull-outs, including overlooks at Goosenecks and the Chimney Rock viewpoint. The road is mostly paved but has some unpaved sections; check conditions if wet. In October, the light enhances the rich colors of the rocks, and fewer visitors mean a more serene experience.
🚗Highway 12 Scenic Byway
Day Trip124-mile loop — allow a full day
Highway 12 is a designated All-American Road that offers a diverse landscape as it connects Capitol Reef with Bryce Canyon. The route features stunning views of red rock canyons, expansive vistas, and the unique geological formations of Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument. Key stops include the Anasazi State Park Museum and the Burr Trail scenic backway, with several overlooks along the way. Allow time for photo stops and short hikes. October provides cooler temperatures and vibrant fall colors, enhancing the scenery.
🚗Goosenecks Overlook
Viewpoint10-min drive from visitor center
Goosenecks Overlook provides a panoramic view of the meandering San Rafael River and its dramatic canyon. The viewpoint showcases the deep, winding gorges carved into the rock. Parking is available at the overlook, but space is limited, so arrive early. The October light casts long shadows over the canyon walls, emphasizing the curves of the river below, making it an ideal time for photography.
🚗Chimney Rock Viewpoint
Viewpoint15-min drive from visitor center
Chimney Rock Viewpoint offers a striking view of the iconic spire rising from the surrounding landscape, framed by the rugged cliffs of Capitol Reef. The viewpoint is easily accessible via a short drive and features a small parking area. The viewpoint is particularly beautiful in October when the light is softer and the surrounding foliage may display fall colors. Expect fewer crowds in the evening, providing a peaceful experience.
