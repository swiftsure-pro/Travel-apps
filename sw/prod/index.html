St. George, Utah

October 17, 2026

Paul Torres

What to Know About St. George, Utah

St. George serves as a one-night stop before Zion National Park. October temperatures allow comfortable daytime hiking with cool nights. Lodging check-in is set for 4 p.m. local time.

Local customs: Many restaurants close by 9 p.m. on weekdays. Gas stations in town offer the last reliable fuel before entering Zion.

Best times of day: Hike Snow Canyon trails before 10 a.m. to avoid afternoon heat and secure parking at popular trailheads.

Transportation quirks: I-15 northbound traffic from Las Vegas can slow near the state line on Friday afternoons. No public transit reaches the dinosaur site or Snow Canyon.

Safety: Carry at least two liters of water per person on any trail. Flash flood risk exists in narrow canyons after distant rain.

Crowd patterns: October sees lighter visitation than spring or summer. Expect more local families on weekends at Snow Canyon.

Local etiquette: Stay on designated trails to protect fragile cryptobiotic soil crusts. Yield to uphill hikers on narrow sections.

What to Know About Zion National Park

Single-day stop on October 18 requires shuttle use inside Zion Canyon. Lodging check-in is at 4:00 PM in Springdale. Fall temperatures swing 30+ degrees between day and night.

Local customs: Shuttle boarding is first-come, first-served; arrive early at stops to secure space.

Best times of day: Early morning shuttle runs have the shortest waits. Late afternoon light improves visibility on east-facing viewpoints.

Transportation quirks: Private vehicles are prohibited on Zion Canyon Scenic Drive after the first shuttle begins running; plan all canyon access around shuttle schedules.

Safety: Flash flood risk persists in The Narrows; obtain current conditions at the visitor center each morning.

Crowd patterns: October sees moderate visitation compared with summer; mid-week days remain quieter than weekends.

Local etiquette: Stay on established trails and boardwalks to protect cryptobiotic soil crusts visible along many paths.

USFWS Mountain-Prairie

What to Know About Bryce Canyon National Park

October daytime temperatures at Bryce Canyon National Park range from the mid-40s to low-50s with nights in the 20s. Snow is possible after mid-month. The main road stays open but the shuttle runs on a reduced schedule.

Local customs: Park entrance fee is collected at the gate; annual passes are accepted. Tipping 15–20% is standard at table-service restaurants.

Best times of day: Early morning before 9 AM gives the best light on the eastern-facing hoodoos. Sunset viewpoints are crowded from 4:30 PM onward.

Transportation quirks: The park shuttle stops at all major viewpoints and trailheads. Parking at Sunset and Bryce Points fills by 10 AM on clear days.

Safety: Trails can be icy after overnight freezes. Carry micro-spikes or Yaktrax after October 15 and check conditions at the visitor center.

Crowd patterns: Visitor numbers drop after Columbus Day. Weekday mornings remain quiet; weekends see more day-trippers from Salt Lake City.

Local etiquette: Stay on designated trails to protect fragile hoodoos. Yield to horses on shared trails and give them space to pass.

Capitol Reef National Park

October 21–22, 2026

Ken Lund from Reno, Nevada, USA

What to Know About Capitol Reef National Park

Capitol Reef sits along UT-24 with lodging in Torrey 11 miles west of the visitor center. October days stay mild but nights cool quickly; water sources on trails are limited.

Local customs: Pack out all trash; fruit from the historic orchards is available for purchase only at the visitor center.

Best times of day: Start hikes by 8 AM to avoid midday heat on slickrock; sunset light on the cliffs lasts until 6:30 PM in late October.

Transportation quirks: Notom-Bullfrog Road requires high-clearance after rain; standard cars stay on paved roads and the Scenic Drive.

Safety: Flash floods can fill Capitol Gorge and slot canyons within minutes; check the visitor center board each morning.

Crowd patterns: Fewer visitors after Columbus Day; parking at Hickman Bridge trailhead remains available past 10 AM.

Local etiquette: Stay on established trails around petroglyph panels; do not touch or chalk the rock surfaces.

What to Know About Moab

Moab in late October has daytime highs near 72°F and nights near 38°F. The three-day window falls after peak summer visitation but before winter closures on higher roads.

Local customs: Most restaurants close by 9pm; plan dinner before 7:30pm on weekdays.

Best times of day: Trailheads are coolest and least crowded before 9am. Sunset light on the cliffs lasts until 6:30pm.

Transportation quirks: US-191 south of town has no shoulder for several miles; cyclists use the dedicated path along the river.

Safety: Carry at least two liters of water on any hike longer than one hour. Afternoon winds can reach 30 mph on exposed rims.

Crowd patterns: Arches and Canyonlands see 30-40% fewer visitors than July. Weekends still fill the main parking lots by mid-morning.

Local etiquette: Stay on designated trails to protect cryptobiotic soil crusts. Yield to uphill hikers and mountain bikers on singletrack.
