Brussels, Belgium

August 31 - September 1, 2026

Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels receives steady visitor traffic through early September. Airport train to city center runs every 15 minutes. Most attractions remain open daily with standard hours.

Local customs: Many cafes close between lunch and dinner service. Street markets operate on specific weekdays only.

Best times of day: Grand Place is quieter before 10am. Museums see fewer visitors after 3pm.

Transportation quirks: Trams and buses require contactless payment or pre-purchased tickets. Taxis at the airport use fixed rates to the center.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded squares and on metro lines. Use hotel safes for passports.

Crowd patterns: Tour groups peak between 11am and 2pm at major landmarks. Evenings remain active around restaurant districts.

Local etiquette: Greet shop staff with a brief hello before asking questions. Tipping is modest and optional at casual spots.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September offers mild weather and reduced summer crowds. Focus on canal walks, museums and market food within a compact center.

Local customs: Bikes have right of way on dedicated lanes. Walk on the sidewalk to avoid collisions.

Best times of day: Mornings before 10am for shorter museum lines. Evenings after 6pm for quieter canal areas.

Transportation quirks: Trams and bikes are faster than driving inside the ring. Use OV-chipkaart or contactless for public transit.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded market and museum areas. Keep bags closed.

Crowd patterns: Fewer visitors than July and August. Weekdays remain busier than weekends at major sites.

Local etiquette: Stand to the right on escalators. Greet shop staff with a simple hello when entering.

What to Know About Berlin, Germany

Berlin in early September offers mild weather suitable for walking between central districts. Public transport is efficient and covers all listed sites. Markets and casual eateries operate normally during the stay.

Local customs: Tipping 5–10% is standard at cafes and restaurants. Cash is preferred at many small food stands.

Best times of day: Mornings before 9am are quieter at Brandenburg Gate and the Reichstag. Late afternoon light suits the East Side Gallery.

Transportation quirks: U-Bahn and S-Bahn run frequently; buy tickets from machines before boarding. Validate tickets on platform machines.

Safety: Watch for bicycle traffic on sidewalks and dedicated lanes. Keep bags secure in crowded areas.

Crowd patterns: Shoulder season means shorter lines than summer at major landmarks. Weekends see more local visitors at markets.

Local etiquette: Stand to the right on escalators. Quiet conversation is expected on public transport.

What to Know About Prague, Czech Republic

Three-day September stop between Berlin and Frankfurt. Shoulder season means moderate crowds at major sites. Focus on cafes, markets and neighborhood spots for meals.

Local customs: Tipping 10 percent is standard at restaurants; round up for small bills.

Best times of day: Early morning visits to Prague Castle and Charles Bridge reduce waiting times.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded squares and on public transport.

Crowd patterns: Tour groups peak mid-morning to mid-afternoon; evenings are quieter.

Local etiquette: Say 'dobrý den' when entering small shops; wait to be seated in restaurants.
