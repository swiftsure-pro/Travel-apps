Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in late summer is warm and bustling, ideal for exploring the city's rich culture and cuisine. Expect moderate crowds and vibrant street life. Local markets and cafes offer affordable dining options.

Local customs: Greetings are often warm; a handshake is common. It's polite to say 'Bonjour' or 'Bonsoir' when entering shops.

Best times of day: Morning is perfect for visiting popular attractions before the crowds arrive. Late afternoon is great for enjoying outdoor cafes.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses connecting key areas. Consider a day pass for convenience.

Safety: Brussels is generally safe, but remain vigilant in crowded areas for pickpockets. Avoid poorly lit streets at night.

Crowd patterns: Weekends see higher foot traffic at major sights. Early mornings or late afternoons are best for quieter visits.

Local etiquette: Politeness is important; use 's'il vous plaît' and 'merci' when interacting. Tipping is not mandatory but appreciated.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is ideal for exploring its cultural heritage and lively neighborhoods. The pleasant weather encourages outdoor activities, including canal walks and park visits.

Local customs: Biking is a popular mode of transport; consider renting a bike to blend in with locals.

Best times of day: Mornings are great for visiting popular attractions before the crowds arrive. Evenings offer a vibrant atmosphere with many cafes and bars open late.

Transportation quirks: Trams and buses are efficient; however, be mindful of bike lanes when walking or crossing the street.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but keep an eye on personal belongings in crowded areas.

Crowd patterns: Expect more visitors at popular sites during weekends, particularly on Saturdays.

Local etiquette: It's customary to greet with a friendly 'Hallo' and to say 'Dank u' when receiving service.

What to Know About Prague, Czech Republic

Prague in September offers mild weather and fewer crowds, ideal for exploring the city's historic sites and vibrant neighborhoods. Expect highs around 72°F and lows around 38°F. Budget dining options are plentiful, from cafes to street food.

Local customs: Czechs appreciate politeness; greet with 'Dobrý den' (Good day) and say 'Děkuji' (Thank you).

Best times of day: Mornings are best for sightseeing to avoid crowds, especially at popular spots like Charles Bridge.

Transportation quirks: Public transportation is efficient; consider getting a travel pass for unlimited rides on trams and buses.

Safety: Prague is generally safe, but watch for pickpockets in crowded areas.

Crowd patterns: Expect busier crowds in popular tourist areas during midday; early mornings are quieter.

Local etiquette: Tipping around 10% is common in restaurants; leave small change for cafes and bars.
