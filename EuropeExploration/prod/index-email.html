Brussels, Belgium

August 31 - September 1, 2026

Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Two-day stop in Brussels focuses on central landmarks and low-cost meals from markets and cafes. Arrival from the airport occurs after 9am on August 31.

Local customs: Many cafes close by 6pm on weekdays. Tap water is safe but rarely served without request.

Best times of day: Mornings before 10am offer shorter lines at major sites. Evening markets operate until 8pm on weekends.

Transportation quirks: Tram and metro require contactless payment or MOBIB card purchase at machines. Driving in the city center uses limited paid parking zones.

Safety: Watch belongings in crowded areas around the Grand Place. Standard EU emergency number is 112.

Crowd patterns: Tourist numbers stay high through early September with peak density midday at the Atomium.

Local etiquette: Greet servers with 'Bonjour' or 'Goedendag' before ordering. Tipping is optional and usually rounded to the nearest euro.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September has mild weather and reduced summer crowds. The city runs on bicycles and trams; plan walking routes around dedicated bike lanes.

Local customs: Cyclists have right of way on marked paths. Do not walk in bike lanes.

Best times of day: Mornings are best for museums before tour groups arrive. Evenings suit canal walks with fewer commuters.

Transportation quirks: Trams and buses require contactless payment or an OV-chipkaart. Taxis are scarce during peak hours.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas near the train station and Dam Square.

Crowd patterns: September sees lighter crowds than July and August. Weekends remain busier around the canal ring.

Local etiquette: Keep right on sidewalks and stairs. Greet shop staff with a brief hello when entering.

What to Know About Berlin, Germany

Berlin in early September has comfortable temperatures for walking between historical districts. The city uses an extensive U-Bahn and S-Bahn network that runs until late.

Local customs: Tipping 5-10% at casual spots is standard; keep coins ready for public restrooms.

Best times of day: Morning visits to major landmarks avoid school groups. Evening light favors the East Side Gallery murals.

Transportation quirks: Buy a 72-hour ticket for unlimited U-Bahn, S-Bahn, tram and bus use. Validate tickets before boarding.

Safety: Watch for bike lanes on sidewalks and keep bags zipped in crowded areas like Alexanderplatz.

Crowd patterns: Fewer visitors than summer but school groups increase after 9am on weekdays.

Local etiquette: Wait for the green pedestrian light even when no traffic is visible. Quiet hours run from 10pm to 6am in residential blocks.
