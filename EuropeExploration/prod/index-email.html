Brussels, Belgium

August 31 - September 1, 2026

Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in late summer is lively and filled with outdoor dining options. Expect mild temperatures averaging around 72°F during the day. This is a popular time for local events and outdoor markets.

Local customs: Belgium has a rich café culture, and locals often enjoy leisurely meals. It's common to greet staff with a simple 'Bonjour' or 'Bonsoir'.

Best times of day: Mornings are ideal for visiting popular attractions to avoid crowds. Late afternoons are also pleasant for outdoor activities.

Transportation quirks: Brussels has an extensive public transport system, including trams and buses. Consider purchasing a day pass for unlimited travel.

Safety: Brussels is generally safe, but be cautious in crowded tourist areas to avoid pickpockets. Keep your belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at major attractions during weekends. Weekdays are typically less busy.

Local etiquette: Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory; rounding up the bill is common. Respecting dining customs, such as waiting for everyone to be served before eating, is important.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is ideal for exploring its rich culture and history. The weather is cool, and tourist crowds are manageable. Focus on neighborhood cafes and local markets for dining.

Local customs: Biking is a primary mode of transportation; be respectful of bike lanes. Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory.

Best times of day: Mornings are less crowded at major attractions. Evening strolls along the canals offer a picturesque view.

Transportation quirks: Trams and buses are reliable for getting around the city. Consider purchasing an OV-chipkaart for easy access.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but keep an eye on personal belongings, especially in crowded areas.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at major attractions during midday. Visiting early or late can make for a more enjoyable experience.

Local etiquette: When in cafes, it's common to seat yourself. Always greet staff with a friendly 'hallo' before ordering.

Ouael Ben Salah

What to Know About Prague, Czech Republic

Prague in early September offers comfortable temperatures and moderate crowds. Visitors can enjoy outdoor attractions and local dining at cafes and markets while avoiding fine dining.

Local customs: Tipping around 10% is customary in restaurants. It's polite to greet staff with a simple 'Dobrý den' (Good day).

Best times of day: Mornings are ideal for visiting popular attractions to avoid crowds. Evenings are perfect for strolling the Old Town and enjoying street performances.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and the metro covering most areas. Tickets must be validated before boarding.

Safety: Prague is generally safe for tourists, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Expect busier crowds near main attractions during the day, especially on weekends. Mornings tend to be less crowded.

Local etiquette: Be respectful in religious sites by dressing modestly. It’s polite to ask for permission before taking photos of people.
