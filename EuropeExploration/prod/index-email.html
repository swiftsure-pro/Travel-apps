What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in late summer offers pleasant weather and a vibrant atmosphere, making it an ideal time for outdoor dining and sightseeing. Expect a mix of tourists and locals as summer crowds begin to diminish.

Local customs: It is common to greet shopkeepers and waitstaff with a polite 'bonjour' or 'bonsoir'. Belgians appreciate good manners, so say 'merci' when receiving service.

Best times of day: Morning is ideal for visiting popular attractions to avoid crowds. Late afternoon is perfect for enjoying outdoor cafes and street performances.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, but trams can be crowded during rush hours. Consider purchasing a multi-day pass for convenience.

Safety: Brussels is generally safe, but remain vigilant in crowded areas to avoid pickpockets. Use common sense, especially in tourist-heavy locations.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at major attractions during weekends. Early mornings and late afternoons are typically quieter times.

Local etiquette: Try to speak a few phrases in French or Dutch as a sign of respect. Eating while walking is less common; most locals prefer to sit down for meals.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is a great time to enjoy the city's outdoor cafes and cultural attractions. The weather is mild, with plenty of activities available. Expect a lively atmosphere as locals and tourists alike enjoy the early fall.

Local customs: Biking is a common mode of transport; be mindful of cyclists when walking. It's polite to greet shopkeepers when entering stores.

Best times of day: Mornings are typically quieter at attractions, making them ideal for visits. Late afternoons often see locals enjoying parks and cafes.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses connecting major sites. Consider getting an OV-chipkaart for easier travel.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Stay aware of your surroundings, especially at night.

Crowd patterns: Weekends can be crowded, especially in popular tourist areas. Visiting attractions early in the morning or late afternoon can help avoid crowds.

Local etiquette: Keep noise levels down in residential areas and public transport. It's customary to say 'thank you' in Dutch ('dank u') when receiving service.

What to Know About Berlin, Germany

Berlin in early September provides mild weather, making it ideal for outdoor exploration. Visitors can enjoy a range of dining options from street food to neighborhood cafés, focusing on affordable choices. The city is less crowded than in peak summer, allowing for a more relaxed experience.

Local customs: Tipping around 10% is appreciated in restaurants and cafes. Always greet with a polite 'Hallo' when entering local shops.

Best times of day: Mornings are best for visiting popular attractions to avoid crowds. Late afternoons are ideal for enjoying outdoor parks.

Transportation quirks: Public transport runs efficiently, with U-Bahn and S-Bahn trains being popular. Consider purchasing a day pass for unlimited travel on all public transport.

Safety: Berlin is generally safe, but remain aware of your surroundings in crowded areas and keep personal belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds on weekends, particularly at major attractions and markets. Weekdays are quieter for sightseeing.

Local etiquette: Public transport requires silence or quiet conversation; loud noises are frowned upon. Avoid discussing sensitive topics like politics in public spaces.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at popular sites during midday. Early mornings or late afternoons offer a quieter experience.

Local etiquette: Respect local customs, such as saying 'thank you' (Děkuji) when receiving service. Avoid loud conversations in public places.
