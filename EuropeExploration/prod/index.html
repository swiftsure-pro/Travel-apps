What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in late summer offers pleasant weather ideal for sightseeing. Expect a mix of cultural experiences, from historical landmarks to vibrant street life. Street food and local markets provide budget-friendly dining options.

Local customs: It is common to greet people with a handshake. French is the primary language, but many locals also speak English.

Best times of day: Mornings are great for exploring popular sites before crowds arrive. Late afternoons often feature local events or street performers.

Transportation quirks: Public transport in Brussels is efficient, with trams and buses connecting major attractions. Consider purchasing a multi-day pass for convenience.

Safety: Brussels is generally safe, but be aware of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep personal belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Late summer sees fewer tourists compared to July, but popular sites can still be busy, especially on weekends.

Local etiquette: Tipping is appreciated, though not mandatory. A small round-up on the bill is common in restaurants.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is ideal for exploring its rich culture and history. Expect comfortable weather for walking, but check for any seasonal closures at attractions.

Local customs: Biking is a primary mode of transport; be mindful of cyclists while walking. Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory.

Best times of day: Mornings are less crowded at major attractions. Early evening offers a vibrant atmosphere in cafes and on the streets.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses running frequently. Purchase an OV-chipkaart for easy travel.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but keep an eye on personal belongings in crowded areas. Be cautious in nightlife districts.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at popular attractions during weekends. Weekdays are more relaxed for sightseeing.

Local etiquette: Respect local customs, such as not walking on bike lanes. It's polite to greet service staff in Dutch with 'Hallo' or 'Goedemorgen'.

Local etiquette: Germans appreciate punctuality. Arrive on time for meals and appointments.

What to Know About Prague, Czech Republic

Prague in September offers mild weather, making it ideal for walking tours and outdoor exploration. Expect a mix of cultural events and fewer crowds compared to summer. Local dining focuses on casual spots featuring traditional Czech food.

Local customs: Tipping is customary in restaurants, typically around 10%. Greet people with a friendly 'Dobrý den' (Good day).

Best times of day: Morning is best for visiting popular attractions to avoid crowds. Late afternoon offers beautiful light for photography.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses serving the city well. Purchase tickets at kiosks or machines before boarding.

Safety: Prague is generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep an eye on your belongings.

Crowd patterns: Crowds are lighter in September, especially at major attractions. Weekdays are less busy than weekends.

Local etiquette: Respect quiet zones in public transport and avoid loud conversations. It's polite to say 'Děkuji' (Thank you) when receiving service.
