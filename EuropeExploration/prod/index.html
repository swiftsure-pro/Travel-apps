Brussels, Belgium

August 31 - September 1, 2026

Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in early September maintains comfortable walking weather with occasional light showers. Two full days allow coverage of the historic center and outer monuments. Public transport and walking cover most sites.

Local customs: Many cafes close kitchens between 2:30 and 6 p.m.; plan meals accordingly.

Best times of day: Mornings before 10 a.m. offer thinner crowds at the Grand Place and Atomium.

Transportation quirks: Trams and buses require contactless payment or pre-purchased MOBIB cards at machines.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in dense areas around the Grand Place and near the Atomium on weekends.

Crowd patterns: Tour groups peak between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m.; early morning visits reduce wait times.

Local etiquette: Greet shop staff with a brief 'Bonjour' or 'Goedendag' before asking questions.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September offers mild weather and shoulder-season crowds. The city center is compact and best explored on foot or by bike. Public transport uses contactless OV-chipkaart or mobile payments.

Local customs: Cyclists have right of way on dedicated lanes; always yield when crossing them. Tipping 5–10% is standard at restaurants.

Best times of day: Mornings before 10am are quietest at major museums. Evenings after 7pm see lighter foot traffic on the canals.

Transportation quirks: Trams and buses require a card or app ticket before boarding; cash is not accepted on board. Central parking garages fill by midday.

Safety: Watch for bike theft and pickpockets in crowded areas like Dam Square. Use hotel safes for passports.

Crowd patterns: September sees fewer large tour groups than summer but weekends remain busy around the canal ring.

Local etiquette: Greet shop staff with a short 'hallo' on entry. Keep voices low on public transport.

What to Know About Berlin, Germany

Berlin in early September offers mild weather suitable for walking between central districts. The city uses an extensive U-Bahn and S-Bahn network with day passes available at machines.

Local customs: Tipping 5-10% is standard at cafes and restaurants. Cash is preferred at many small food stands.

Best times of day: Mornings before 10am have shorter lines at the Reichstag dome. Evenings after 7pm bring fewer crowds along Unter den Linden.

Transportation quirks: Validate all tickets before boarding. Bikes have right of way on many paths.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded tourist areas around the Brandenburg Gate. Use official taxi stands at stations.

Crowd patterns: Fewer visitors than July and August but still busy on weekends around major landmarks.

Local etiquette: Greet staff with a simple 'Hallo' when entering small shops or cafes.

What to Know About Prague, Czech Republic

Prague in early September has mild days and cool nights. Three days allows time for the main historic core plus one hilltop outing.

Local customs: Trams require ticket validation at machines before boarding. Tipping 10 percent is standard at restaurants.

Best times of day: Charles Bridge clears after 7pm. Castle grounds open at 9am with shortest lines before 10am.

Transportation quirks: Metro and tram tickets are sold at machines and newsstands. Validate on every ride.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in dense areas around the bridge and square. Use hotel safes for passports.

Crowd patterns: Tour groups peak between 10am and 4pm. Early morning and after 6pm are quieter.

Local etiquette: Greet servers with dobry den when entering. Pay at the table when asked rather than at the bar.
