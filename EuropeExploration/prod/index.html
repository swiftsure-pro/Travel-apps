Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in late summer is a lively city with mild temperatures and bustling outdoor markets. Expect a mix of cultural attractions, local cuisine, and vibrant street life during your two-day stay.

Local customs: Belgians appreciate politeness; a simple 'bonjour' or 'merci' goes a long way. Tipping is customary but not required; round up the bill in casual settings.

Best times of day: Mornings are great for exploring markets and cafés, while evenings can offer vibrant nightlife and dining experiences.

Transportation quirks: The public transport system is efficient, with trams, buses, and metro services. Validate tickets before use to avoid fines.

Safety: Brussels is generally safe but stay aware of your surroundings, especially in crowded areas. Keep valuables secure and avoid poorly lit areas at night.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at major attractions on weekends and during peak hours. Early mornings or late afternoons are best for quieter visits.

Local etiquette: Respect local customs by following basic dining etiquette, such as waiting for everyone to be served before starting a meal. Avoid loud conversations in public spaces.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is a great time to explore the city with mild weather and fewer crowds. Expect a mix of cultural activities and outdoor experiences as fall sets in.

Local customs: Bicycles are a primary mode of transport; be aware of bike lanes and signals. It's common to greet shopkeepers when entering and leaving stores.

Best times of day: Early mornings are ideal for visiting popular attractions before crowds arrive. Late afternoons provide a relaxed vibe in parks and cafes.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses that run frequently. Consider getting an OV-chipkaart for easy access to public transport.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep an eye on personal belongings, especially in markets.

Crowd patterns: September sees a decrease in tourists compared to summer, making it easier to navigate attractions. However, weekends can still be busy.

Local etiquette: When dining, it's polite to wait for the host to indicate where to sit. Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory; rounding up the bill is common.

Crowd patterns: Expect more tourists on weekends, especially at major attractions. Weekdays are quieter for sightseeing.

Local etiquette: It's customary to greet shopkeepers and waitstaff. Speaking a few phrases in German is appreciated.
