Brussels, Belgium

August 31 - September 1, 2026

Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in late summer is warm and lively, with plenty of outdoor activities. Expect casual dining options, especially in cafes and markets. The city is less crowded than during peak season, making it an enjoyable experience.

Local customs: It is customary to greet locals with a polite 'Bonjour' or 'Bonsoir'. Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory; rounding up the bill is common.

Best times of day: Mornings are great for visiting popular attractions before crowds build. Late afternoons provide excellent light for photography, especially in the Grand Place.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams, buses, and metro systems. Consider buying a day pass for unlimited travel.

Safety: Brussels is generally safe, but be aware of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep valuables secured and stay vigilant.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at key attractions during weekends. Evenings can also be busy, especially in dining areas.

Local etiquette: Try to use French or Dutch phrases when speaking with locals. Respect for personal space is important, so maintain a comfortable distance.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is ideal for exploring its rich culture and history. Expect pleasant weather and fewer tourists than the summer months. Focus on local cafes and markets for budget-friendly meals.

Local customs: Bicycles are a primary mode of transportation; be mindful of bike lanes. Greeting locals with 'Hallo' is appreciated.

Best times of day: Mornings are quieter, making them perfect for museum visits. Evenings are vibrant with outdoor dining options.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses connecting all major areas. Consider purchasing an OV-chipkaart for seamless travel.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep personal belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at major attractions during midday. Visiting in the early morning or late afternoon can help avoid lines.

Local etiquette: When dining, it's customary to wait for everyone to be served before starting. Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory.

What to Know About Berlin, Germany

Berlin in early September offers mild weather, perfect for exploring the city on foot. The atmosphere is lively with various events still happening, but expect fewer tourists than in peak summer. Keep an eye on local event calendars for specific happenings.

Local customs: Germans value punctuality, so try to be on time for any scheduled activities or reservations. Greetings are often formal; a simple 'Guten Tag' is appreciated.

Best times of day: Mornings are ideal for visiting popular attractions before crowds build up. Late afternoons can be great for leisurely strolls and outdoor dining.

Transportation quirks: Berlin has an extensive public transport system. The U-Bahn and S-Bahn are reliable, and tickets must be purchased before boarding. Biking is also a popular way to get around.

Safety: Berlin is generally safe, but remain aware of your surroundings, especially in crowded areas. Keep your belongings secure and avoid poorly lit areas at night.

Crowd patterns: Weekends can be busier at major attractions, while weekdays tend to be quieter. Early September sees a mix of tourists and locals enjoying the city.

Local etiquette: Tipping is customary, usually around 10% of the bill. When using public transport, be courteous and give up your seat for those in need.
