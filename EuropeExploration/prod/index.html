Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels offers a mix of history, culture, and cuisine during your late summer visit. Expect warm days and cooler evenings, ideal for exploring outdoor markets and cafes. Focus on local spots for budget-friendly dining.

Local customs: Belgians appreciate politeness; greeting with a friendly 'bonjour' or 'goede dag' is common. Tipping is not obligatory but rounding up the bill is appreciated.

Best times of day: Mornings are best for visiting major attractions to avoid crowds. Late afternoons provide a pleasant time for outdoor dining.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is reliable and well-connected. Consider purchasing a day pass for unlimited travel on trams, buses, and metro.

Safety: Brussels is generally safe, but be aware of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep belongings secured, especially in tourist spots.

Crowd patterns: August sees moderate tourism, with fewer crowds compared to peak summer months. Expect busier weekends, especially in central areas.

Local etiquette: Respect local dining hours; many restaurants open later in the evening. It’s customary to wait for the host to show you to your table.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is pleasantly mild, ideal for exploring the city's rich cultural offerings. The combination of historical sites, parks, and vibrant neighborhoods makes it a great time to visit. Budget-conscious travelers will find numerous affordable dining options throughout the city.

Local customs: Biking is a primary mode of transport; pedestrians should be cautious of cyclists. Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory in casual dining.

Best times of day: Mornings and late afternoons are best for sightseeing to avoid crowds. Evening strolls along the canals are particularly enjoyable.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses widely available. Consider purchasing an OV chip card for convenience.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but remain vigilant in crowded areas for pickpockets. Use well-lit paths when walking at night.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at major attractions during weekends. Weekdays are typically quieter, making for a better experience.

Local etiquette: Respect local customs by adhering to bike lanes and being courteous to others. Always greet locals with a friendly 'Hallo.'

Prague in September offers cool, pleasant weather and a rich cultural scene. Explore outdoor sites while enjoying lighter crowds compared to summer. Look for local cafes and markets for budget-friendly dining options.

Best times of day: Morning visits to major attractions help avoid crowds. Early evenings are great for dining and local entertainment.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, with trams and metro systems. Validate tickets before boarding to avoid fines.

Safety: Prague is generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep personal belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Expect busier crowds at major attractions during midday. Early mornings and late afternoons are quieter times.

Local etiquette: Respect local customs by speaking basic Czech phrases. Avoid discussing politics or sensitive historical topics.

What to Know About Frankfurt, Germany

Frankfurt in September is comfortable for exploring, with a mix of historic charm and modern flair. The city is pedestrian-friendly, making it easy to get around on foot or by public transport.

Local customs: Tipping is customary, typically rounding up the bill or adding 5-10%. Try local specialties like Apfelwein.

Best times of day: Mornings are ideal for visiting attractions before crowds arrive. Evenings offer a lively nightlife scene.

Transportation quirks: Public transportation is efficient, but be sure to validate tickets before boarding. Cycling is also popular with dedicated lanes.

Safety: Frankfurt is generally safe, but remain aware of your surroundings, especially in crowded areas.

Crowd patterns: September sees moderate crowds as summer tourists diminish. Popular sites may still be busy on weekends.

Local etiquette: Greet shopkeepers and waitstaff with a polite 'Guten Tag.' It's customary to wait for the host to seat you in restaurants.
