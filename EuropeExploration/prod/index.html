What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels in early September maintains summer warmth with long daylight hours. The two-day window fits between rail legs on the five-capitals route.

Local customs: Many cafes close between 2 p.m. and 5 p.m. on weekdays.

Best times of day: Morning hours before 10 a.m. provide lighter crowds at the Grand Place.

Transportation quirks: Trams and metro require contactless payment or a MOBIB card at station machines.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded squares and on the metro during peak afternoon hours.

Crowd patterns: Tourist density stays high through the first week of September then declines.

Local etiquette: Greet shop staff with a brief bonjour or goedemorgen before asking questions.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September has mild weather and moderate crowds. The city center is compact and best explored on foot or by tram. Canals and bike paths require constant attention to traffic.

Local customs: Cyclists have right of way on dedicated paths. Walk on the sidewalk, not the bike lane.

Best times of day: Mornings before 10am are quietest at major museums. Evenings after 6pm bring fewer tour groups along the canals.

Transportation quirks: Trams run on tracks shared with cars in places. Buy an OV-chipkaart or use contactless payment before boarding.

Safety: Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas near the train station and Dam Square. Keep bags closed and in front.

Crowd patterns: Shoulder season means shorter lines than summer but weekends still busy at Anne Frank House and Rijksmuseum.

Local etiquette: Greet shop staff with a brief hello. Tipping 5-10% is standard at casual restaurants.
