Brussels, Belgium

Stephanie LeBlanc

What to Know About Brussels, Belgium

Brussels is a vibrant city known for its rich history, architecture, and cuisine. In late summer, enjoy outdoor cafes and local markets. Expect crowds, but not overwhelming, as many tourists begin to leave.

Local customs: It is customary to greet with a handshake and to use 'Bonjour' or 'Bonsoir'. Tipping is appreciated, but not mandatory.

Best times of day: Mornings are great for exploring markets, while evenings offer lively atmospheres at cafes and restaurants.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient, but be prepared for occasional strikes. Biking is popular, with many dedicated lanes.

Safety: Keep an eye on your belongings in crowded areas. Brussels is generally safe, but petty theft can occur.

Crowd patterns: Expect moderate crowds in popular areas during the day, especially near attractions. Evenings can be busier in dining districts.

Local etiquette: Be polite and patient, especially in shops and restaurants. Speaking a few phrases in French or Dutch is appreciated.

What to Know About Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam in early September is ideal for exploring with milder weather and fewer tourists than in peak summer. Focus on local cafés and markets for affordable dining options.

Local customs: Engaging in conversation is common; locals appreciate directness. Tipping is not compulsory but rounding up the bill is appreciated.

Best times of day: Mornings are less crowded at major attractions, making it a good time to visit museums. Late afternoons are ideal for strolling along the canals.

Transportation quirks: Cycling is a popular mode of transport; consider renting a bike. Public transport is efficient, with trams and buses readily available.

Safety: Amsterdam is generally safe, but be mindful of pickpockets in crowded areas. Always secure your belongings.

Crowd patterns: Expect busier crowds on weekends, especially at popular tourist sites. Weekdays are typically quieter for sightseeing.

Local etiquette: Be respectful of local customs and personal space. When cycling, follow traffic rules and be aware of pedestrians.

What to Know About Berlin, Germany

Berlin in early September is lively with a mix of cultural events and pleasant weather. Expect a range of dining options from street food to casual cafes. Most attractions are accessible and public transport is efficient.

Local customs: Germans value punctuality, so be on time for reservations or tours. It's common to greet shopkeepers when entering shops.

Best times of day: Morning hours are ideal for visiting popular attractions to avoid crowds. Late afternoons are great for enjoying parks and outdoor cafes.

Transportation quirks: Public transportation is extensive and reliable, but be aware that some trains may not run as frequently on weekends.

Safety: Berlin is generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep personal belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Popular tourist sites can get crowded during the day, especially on weekends. Early mornings or late afternoons are best for visits.

Local etiquette: It's polite to say 'Danke' (thank you) after receiving service. Tipping is customary but not obligatory; rounding up is appreciated.

What to Know About Prague, Czech Republic

Prague in September features mild weather and fewer tourists compared to summer months. Expect to enjoy outdoor attractions and local markets. Dining options focus on casual and budget-friendly experiences.

Local customs: Tipping is customary in restaurants, typically around 10%. It's polite to greet with 'Dobrý den' (Good day) when entering shops or restaurants.

Best times of day: Mornings are ideal for visiting popular attractions to avoid crowds. Evenings offer a lively atmosphere with local events and performances.

Transportation quirks: Public transport is efficient and includes trams, buses, and the metro. Purchase tickets at kiosks or machines before boarding.

Safety: Prague is generally safe, but remain vigilant against pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep personal belongings secure.

Crowd patterns: Expect larger crowds at major attractions during weekends. Early weekdays are quieter for exploring.

Local etiquette: Respect local customs, such as not speaking loudly in public spaces. It's appreciated when visitors attempt to use simple Czech phrases.
